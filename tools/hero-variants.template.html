Four ways to put type on top of footage.

Same video, same layout, same copy — only the surface between the two changes. Click through 01–04 above and judge one thing: can you read the name and the lede without the cuts pulling your eye away, and does the treatment still look like a costume designer's site rather than a video player?

01

Grain

细颗粒 + 暗角

No pattern at all. The footage is graded down hard, then covered in film grain and a heavy vignette. Quietest of the four, and the one that ages best — but it leans on darkness alone to hold the type.

02

Halftone

印刷网点

A 3.4px dot screen prints the footage like a newspaper photo or an LED wall. Strongest separation of the four and the most obviously designed — the video reads as an image, not a clip.

03

Weave

织物斜纹

A diagonal cross-hatch, like looking through scrim or a loosely woven gauze. Same job as the halftone, but the reference is the costume shop instead of the print shop.

04

Scrim

磨砂玻璃

The only one that keeps the footage bright. A frosted glass panel is blurred in behind the type and feathered out to the right, so your work stays visible where there is no text on it.
